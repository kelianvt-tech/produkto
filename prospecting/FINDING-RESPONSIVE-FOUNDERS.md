# Finding founders who will actually reply

**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Filter:** Shopify, sells primarily through their own store. **Category irrelevant.**
**Optimising for:** probability of a reply, not fit with the audit tool.

---

## The core trick

**Target people who have already been interviewed.**

Someone who went on a podcast has publicly demonstrated the exact behaviour you need: they said yes to a stranger who wanted to ask them questions for an hour, about their business, for no money.

That is a stronger predictor of "will reply to Kelian" than revenue, category, SKU count or anything else in `ICP.md`. And guest lists are public, free, and come with the founder's name, the brand, and usually their socials.

Compare the two cold emails:

- To a random store: *who are you and why are you in my inbox*
- To a past podcast guest: *"I heard you on Shopify Masters talking about X"* — instant context, instant proof you did homework, and a person who already enjoys talking about their business

## Where the guest lists are

### Shopify's own podcast — start here

**[shopify.com/blog/topics/podcasts](https://www.shopify.com/blog/topics/podcasts)**

Every guest is a Shopify merchant, by definition. Episodes are written up as articles with the brand name, the founder's name and the story. ~600 episodes on Shopify Masters, and the blog index goes back years across multiple pages.

This is the single best source available to you and it costs nothing.

### Other interview-led shows with public guest lists

| Show | Host | Why it's useful |
|---|---|---|
| **eCommerce Conversations** | Eric Bandholz (Beardbrand) | Wide category spread — skincare, food, clothing, home goods |
| **My Wife Quit Her Job** | Steve Chou | **Every guest is bootstrapped, no VC.** Smaller, founder-led, most likely to reply |
| **Unofficial Shopify Podcast** | Kurt Elster | Decade of Shopify store owners |
| **eCommerceFuel** | Andrew Youderian | 5–9 figure DTC operators |
| **Limited Supply** | Nik Sharma | Bigger names, lower reply odds, useful for mapping |

**My Wife Quit Her Job is the sleeper.** Bootstrapped founders have no PR person, no gatekeeper, and answer their own email.

### Beyond podcasts

- **r/shopify and r/ecommerce power posters** — people who write long helpful answers are, by revealed preference, people who like helping strangers. Check post history, DM the good ones.
- **Indie Hackers** — founders who publish revenue numbers voluntarily. Extremely reply-prone.
- **LinkedIn and X build-in-public founders** — anyone posting about their store weekly is reachable and likes attention.
- **Shopify case studies** — [shopify.com/case-studies](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies), same logic as podcasts.
- **Trade press profiles** — Modern Retail, Retail Brew, 2PM founder interviews.

## The responsiveness scorecard

Score each prospect out of 6. Contact 5s and 6s first. Category is not on this list on purpose.

| Signal | Why |
|---|---|
| ☐ Been a podcast guest | Already says yes to strangers with questions |
| ☐ Founder is publicly named and visible | No gatekeeper |
| ☐ Posts regularly on LinkedIn, X or IG | Active people answer messages |
| ☐ Under ~30 people, no careers page | Founder still does everything |
| ☐ Under ~8 years old | Still hungry |
| ☐ Bootstrapped, no VC | No board, no comms policy, decides alone |

**Disqualifying signals:** a press@ address, a PR agency, a careers page with ten roles, an investor page. Those brands have processes, and processes eat cold emails.

## Getting the actual contact

### Email — the pattern

Most small DTC brands use one of these:

```
firstname@brand.com          ← most common
firstname.lastname@brand.com
hello@ / info@               ← shared inbox, last resort
```

Verify before sending. Bounces damage your domain and you only have one.

- **Hunter.io** — free tier, finds and verifies by domain
- **Apollo.io** — free tier, contact data plus verification
- **Snov.io / Findymail / Clearbit** — same idea

Limits change; check current ones. For 20–30 contacts you can stay on free tiers.

### The one most people miss

**Podcast show notes often contain the founder's email or direct socials**, because the guest wanted listeners to reach them. Read the notes before paying for any tool.

### Which channel

| Situation | Channel |
|---|---|
| Bootstrapped, founder-led | Email — they read their own |
| Instagram-heavy brand | IG DM. Founder often reads them personally. |
| Active LinkedIn poster | LinkedIn note. They're already in the app. |
| Reddit/Indie Hackers person | Reply publicly first, then DM |
| Warm intro exists | The intro. Always. |

Don't pay for InMail. If you can't reach someone free, they're not a priority.

## The message — podcast version

Highest-converting variant, because the opening line is proof you're not mass-mailing.

> **Subject:** Your {Podcast} episode — quick question
>
> Hi {Name},
>
> I heard you on {Podcast} talking about {specific thing they said}. {One genuine sentence about why it stuck.}
>
> I'm researching something adjacent and I think you'd have a view. Buyers are starting to ask ChatGPT for products instead of Googling — and most product pages don't state the facts an AI needs to recommend them. I've built a tool that measures it.
>
> I'm not selling anything. I'm trying to work out whether this is a real problem for brands or just an interesting one, and I'd rather ask people who actually run stores than guess.
>
> 20 minutes? Either way I'm happy to run the audit on {Brand} and send you the report, free, nothing attached.
>
> Kelian
> Barcelona · {link}

**Non-negotiable:** {specific thing they said} must be real. Listen to ten minutes of the episode or read the write-up. Fake specificity is worse than none — these people get pitched constantly and they can smell it.

## Realistic plan

| Day | Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Page through the Shopify podcast index. Build a list of 40 brands. |
| 2 | Score all 40 on the six signals. Keep the 5s and 6s. |
| 3 | Find founder names — About page, LinkedIn, show notes. |
| 4 | Find and verify emails. Note which are better reached on IG. |
| 5 | Skim one episode each for the top 20. Write 20 personalised openers. |
| 6 | Send. One at a time, by hand, no sequencer. |
| 13 | One follow-up, two lines. Then stop. |

**Expect 4–8 replies from 20 well-personalised messages to podcast guests.** That's several times the rate of true cold outreach, and it's most of your fifteen conversations from one channel.

Run the Reddit and Instagram channels alongside it and you're there in three weeks.

---

## What not to do

- Don't filter by category. You have no evidence about which category cares most — that's what these conversations are for.
- Don't wait for the audit to be ready for their category. The interview doesn't need it. Offer the report as a gift, not a ticket.
- Don't use a sequencer at twenty contacts. Hand-sent mail doesn't look like a campaign because it isn't one.
- Don't pitch. The moment it becomes a sales call you lose the honest answer, which is the only thing you're actually there for.

# How to actually find them, and what to say

**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Goal:** fifteen conversations with DTC founders. Not sales calls — research interviews.
**Time:** roughly 4 hours to build a contact list of 20–30. Two to three weeks to hold the conversations.

---

## The three separate problems

People conflate these and then get stuck. They're different jobs with different tools.

1. **Find the brand** — which companies fit
2. **Find the person** — who to talk to at that company
3. **Find the contact** — how to reach them

---

## 1. Finding the brands

Ranked by effort-to-yield for your situation.

### Trade show exhibitor directories — free, and the best one nobody uses

Maison&Objet (Paris), Salone del Mobile (Milan), Casa Decor (Madrid), Feria Hábitat (Valencia) all publish **searchable exhibitor lists online**, usually with company name, country, category and often a website and contact.

That's hundreds of furniture brands, pre-filtered to people who spend money on being discovered, sorted by category, free, no tool required. Start here.

You don't need to attend to use the directory. But if you can attend one, do — your entire ICP is in one building and you can run the audit on your phone while standing at their stand.

### Your dad's competitors and suppliers

M&O Living's competitive set *is* your target list, and your dad can name it from memory. Same for suppliers, who sell to dozens of brands and know all of them.

This is the highest-quality source you have and it costs one conversation.

### Store directories — paid, most systematic

- **Store Leads** (storeleads.app) — filter Shopify stores by category, country, product count, traffic estimate, apps installed. Closest thing to a purpose-built tool for this. Paid; check current pricing, but a single month is enough to export a list you'll use for a year.
- **BuiltWith** — similar, tech-stack led.
- **Shopify's own store directory** and `site:myshopify.com` Google searches — free, scrappier.

Worth one month's subscription. Not worth a year's before you have a customer.

### The reverse-engineered route

Already in `ICP.md` and still the cleverest one:

1. Ask ChatGPT ten spec-shaped furniture queries — *"modular sofa with removable covers for a covered terrace, under €2,000"*
2. Note which brands get recommended
3. Find the brands in that category that **don't** get recommended

Those are your targets, and the cold email writes itself because you can name who's beating them. That comparison is the most persuasive sentence available to you and nobody else can send it.

### Instagram and Pinterest

Furniture brands live on Instagram. Follow the category hashtags, look at who Instagram suggests after you follow one brand — the "similar accounts" graph is a genuinely good discovery engine for this category, and it surfaces smaller brands the directories miss.

## 2. Finding the person

At a 20-person DTC brand this is usually the founder, and they're usually easy to find.

| Where | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Their own About / Our Story page** | Small brands almost always name and photograph the founder. Check here first — it's free and takes ten seconds. |
| **LinkedIn search** | Filter by company, look for Founder / Co-founder / Head of Ecommerce. Free LinkedIn is fine at this volume; Sales Navigator only if you scale up. |
| **Instagram** | Founder-led brands often link the founder's personal account, or the founder appears in Stories. |
| **Company registries** | Companies House (UK), Registro Mercantil (ES) — director names, free. |

**Who to target by size:** under ~$3M it's the founder, and they decide alone. Above that it's more likely Head of Ecommerce or Marketing, who needs something to show upward. `ICP.md` has this already.

## 3. Finding the contact

### Email — guess and verify

Most small DTC brands use a predictable pattern. In rough order of likelihood:

```
firstname@brand.com
firstname.lastname@brand.com
f.lastname@brand.com
hello@ / info@  ← last resort, goes to a shared inbox
```

**Verify before sending.** Bounces damage your sending reputation and you only get one domain.

- **Hunter.io** — finds and verifies emails by domain. Free tier covers a small batch; enough for 20 prospects.
- **Apollo.io** — free tier includes contact data and email verification.
- **Clearbit / Snov.io / Findymail** — same category, similar free tiers.

Check current limits on each; they change. For 20–30 contacts you can do this entirely on free tiers by rotating.

### Instagram DM — underrated for this category

For founder-led furniture and lifestyle brands, the founder often reads the brand's DMs personally. Response rates can beat email, and it's less formal, which suits a research request.

Keep it to three lines. Don't attach anything. Ask if you can email the report.

### LinkedIn

Best for Head of Ecommerce at slightly larger brands. Connection request with a short note — you get limited characters, so lead with the ask, not the context.

Don't pay for InMail. If you can't reach them free, they're not the priority.

### Which channel, by brand shape

| Brand | Best channel |
|---|---|
| Founder-led, under 20 people | Email or Instagram DM |
| Instagram-heavy, lifestyle | Instagram DM |
| 20+ people, a Head of Ecommerce exists | LinkedIn, then email |
| Warm intro available | **Always the intro. Nothing beats it.** |

## The scripts

### A. The interview request — email

This is the main one. Note what it does *not* do: pitch, attach a deck, ask for a demo, or mention pricing.

> **Subject:** Quick question about {Brand} and AI search
>
> Hi {Name},
>
> I'm researching how DTC brands are handling AI search — the fact that buyers are starting to ask ChatGPT for products instead of Googling them.
>
> I'm not selling anything. I've built a tool that audits whether a catalog states the things AI assistants look for, and I'm trying to work out whether this is a real problem for brands or just an interesting one.
>
> Could I ask you 20 minutes of questions? I'd rather hear what's actually on your plate than guess at it.
>
> Either way, I'm happy to run the audit on {Brand} and send you the report — it's free and there's nothing attached to it.
>
> Kelian
> {city} · {link}

**Why it works:** the ask is small and specific (20 minutes, questions, not a demo). "I'm not selling anything" is true and disarming. Admitting you don't know whether the problem is real makes you a researcher rather than a vendor, and people help researchers. The free audit is a genuine gift with no hook.

### B. Interview request — Instagram DM

> Hi {Name} — not a pitch, promise.
>
> I'm researching how furniture brands are dealing with AI search (people asking ChatGPT for products instead of Googling). Trying to figure out if it's a real problem or just a hyped one.
>
> Could I ask you a few questions sometime? Happy to run a free audit of your catalog either way and send it over — no strings.

### C. LinkedIn connection note

Short — you have very few characters.

> Researching how DTC brands are handling AI search. Not selling anything — would value 20 min of your time, and happy to send a free audit of your catalog either way.

### D. The audit-first version

For when you've already run the report. Stronger, because you lead with something they don't know.

> **Subject:** {Brand} — 6 attributes missing from all 1,240 products
>
> Hi {Name},
>
> I ran an audit across {Brand}'s catalog this week — {N} products, scored on whether the pages state the facts an AI assistant needs to recommend a product.
>
> {One specific finding.} So a question like "{realistic buyer query}" has nothing in your catalog to match against.
>
> Report's attached. Free, nothing attached to it — I'm building in this space and running audits to find out whether this is a problem anyone actually cares about.
>
> If it's useful I'd value 20 minutes to hear whether it matches what you're seeing. If not, no reply needed.
>
> Kelian

### E. The ask to your dad

Don't make this a business meeting. The framing matters — you want names, not endorsement.

> "I'm trying to talk to people who run their own online shop — not to sell them anything, I just need to ask them questions about how they handle product information. Doesn't matter if they're competitors, actually competitors are better. Who do you know? And would you mind sending a two-line intro to a few of them?"

**What you want from him:**

- Names of Barcelona/Spanish furniture brands selling direct
- Suppliers who sell to lots of brands (they know everyone)
- Anyone he's met at Maison&Objet, Salone or Hábitat
- Two-line email intros — those convert far better than you writing cold

**One rule:** these people know your dad. If you sell to them, it lands on him. Ask questions, send the report, don't pitch.

---

## Follow-up

One follow-up, seven days later, two lines. Then stop.

> Hi {Name} — just floating this back to the top of your inbox in case it got buried. Happy to send the audit either way, no reply needed.

Never a third.

## Realistic yield

| Channel | Contacts | Expected conversations |
|---|---|---|
| Warm intros via Dad | 8–10 | 5 |
| Trade show directory outreach | 20 | 2–3 |
| Cold audits | 20 | 2–4 |
| Instagram DMs | 15 | 2–3 |
| Agencies | 5 | 1–2 |
| Reddit / communities | — | 1–2 |

Roughly fifteen if you run several in parallel. Any single channel alone will not get you there — that's the main mistake to avoid.

## What to log, every single time

Do this immediately after each call, not at the end of the week.

- **Exact phrasing** they used for the problem — verbatim, not paraphrased. This fills the empty section in `ICP.md` and becomes every subject line you write afterwards.
- How they phrase **product-level vs brand-level** thinking. That they think product-level is settled (`context/ICP.md`) — capture the wording, not the verdict. The single most important thing you're listening for is now whether they'd **pay**, and what they've already paid for.
- What they've **already spent money on** for this.
- Whether they **asked what it costs**, unprompted.
- Whether they **forwarded the report** internally.

The last two are the only real buying signals. Everything else is politeness.

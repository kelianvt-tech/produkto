# Day 15 review: 18 drafts on Clubhouse Skin, read by hand

15 October 2026. Every draft the agent could write for Clubhouse Skin's open Opportunities (18; the 6-product catalog has no more), on a throwaway copy of the server database. 11 product page fixes, 3 buying guides, 2 comparison pages, 2 FAQs. Cost: 4.3¢ for all 18.

## What was wrong, and what changed

| # | Problem found | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spec tables had junk rows: "Brand: Clubhouse Skin", "Inspiration: the Spanish coast", "Weight: 92 g" | Junk labels and values over 60 characters are removed mechanically |
| 2 | That "Weight" is Shopify's per-variant **shipping weight** (package included). A buyer reads "92 g" as the amount of product: misleading | Relabelled "Shipping weight" in `catalog_facts.py` and never shown to the model |
| 3 | Fact check flagged brand-voice lines ("Considered enough to earn its place on your shelf") and asked the merchant nonsense ("Does the oil earn a place in a routine?") | New verdict "voice": tone with nothing checkable is allowed. "Stated" now covers the product's description too, reworded or combined |
| 4 | Numbered lists broke: "2." and "3." flagged as unstated numbers | List markers ignored; the sentence splitter no longer splits after "2." |
| 5 | An FAQ answer starting "No." was flagged on its own ("What fact should replace 'No.'?") | One- or two-word answers are checked together with the sentence after them |
| 6 | Rhetorical questions ("Looking for a lightweight moisturiser?") flagged as claims | Questions are skipped: they claim nothing |
| 7 | The same product got 3 page fixes from 3 Opportunities (hat, face oil) | "Draft fix" now writes a different type (e.g. FAQ) or opens the page's existing draft instead of a rival rewrite |
| 8 | Comparison tables mostly "Not stated" | A row needs at least two products stating it |
| 9 | Meta-writing: "its description says…", "the product information should make clear…", "under the stated refund policy", "is described as" | Banned in all four prompts, and rewritten mechanically after drafting (the model kept doing it despite the rule) |
| 10 | Returns policy inside a product description | Policies only in an FAQ answer to a returns or shipping question |

## What was good
- No invented facts in any of the 18. Every claim traced to the store's own pages or setup answers.
- Spec tables after the fix are short and buyer-shaped (Material, Fit, Key ingredient, What's included).
- Guides and comparisons answer the actual buyer question, in the brand's voice (short, British spelling, no hype).
- The returns FAQ came from the merchant brief's "objections", and used the store's real policy.

## Still open (not blocking a demo)
- The fact check still holds back some true-but-reworded sentences (about 1 in 2 drafts has one gap). Strict is the right side to err on; the merchant fills or drops the gap in one click.
- Before/after re-run: gaps went from 9 to 9, but the questions are now real ones (e.g. "Is the set intended to brighten skin?") instead of nonsense.
- A small catalog runs out of new work fast: 18 drafts covered every Opportunity. Larger catalogs won't.

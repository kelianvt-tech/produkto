Why fewer steps work better

Most skin doesn't need a long list of products. It needs to be cleaned without being stripped, kept hydrated, and protected from the day. Every extra step adds another formula to your face and another thing to remember, and long routines are the ones that get abandoned after a fortnight.

A short routine also makes it easier to tell what's working. If your skin changes, you only have three products to think about, not ten. Our routine is built on that idea: three products formulated to work in sequence, rather than competing with each other.

Step 1

Cleanse without stripping

Cleansing comes first because everything after it works better on clean skin. The aim is to remove what doesn't belong (the day's grime, sunscreen, excess oil) without stripping away the oils your skin produces to protect itself. Skin that feels tight and squeaky after washing has usually lost more than it needed to.

Look for a cleanser that leaves your face feeling comfortable, not tight. Use lukewarm rather than hot water, massage it in for around thirty seconds, rinse and pat dry.

Step 2

Protect and brighten with vitamin C

Vitamin C is one of the most widely used skincare ingredients for a reason: it's an antioxidant, which means it helps defend skin against environmental damage from things like pollution and sun exposure. Used regularly, it's also associated with a brighter, more even-looking complexion.

A face oil is a comfortable way to use it every day, as long as it's light enough to sink in. A few drops warmed between your fingertips and pressed onto clean skin is enough. Many people prefer vitamin C in the morning, since that's when skin meets the most pollution and daylight.

Step 3

Lock it in with a lightweight moisturiser

Moisturiser goes last. It holds water in the skin and supports the barrier that keeps irritants out, so it seals in everything underneath. If you've avoided moisturiser because it feels greasy or heavy, the answer is usually a lighter texture, not skipping the step.

Ingredients to look for: humectants that attract and hold water, and barrier-supporting ingredients such as niacinamide and squalane. Apply a small amount to your face and neck while your skin is still slightly damp from the steps before.

Questions people ask

What order should skincare go in?

For this routine: cleanser first, then the vitamin-C oil on clean skin, then moisturiser to lock everything in. In the morning, sunscreen goes on last.

Do I need a face oil and a moisturiser?

They do different jobs. The oil here delivers vitamin C and a natural glow; the moisturiser holds hydration in and supports your skin's barrier. If you only want two steps, keep the cleanser and moisturiser and add the oil later.

Is a three-step routine enough?

For most people, yes. Cleansing, protecting and moisturising cover the fundamentals. If you have a specific skin condition, a dermatologist can tell you whether you need anything more.

How long until I see a difference?

Skin renews itself over several weeks, so give any new routine about a month of daily use before judging it. Consistency matters more than the number of products.

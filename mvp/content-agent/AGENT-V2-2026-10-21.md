# The Catalog Optimization Agent, v2: built to move visibility

*21 Oct 2026. Kelian: "an extremely effective agent… it changes everything that needs to be made… because I want my customers to stay with me." This is the design. Nothing here is built yet unless marked **built**.*

## The one idea

An agent is only as good as its loop: **diagnose → change → measure → learn → change again.** A tool that writes good pages once is a copywriter; customers cancel once the pages are written. A tool that keeps finding why a competitor wins a question, changes something, checks whether it worked, and tries something else when it didn't is what keeps them paying.

Nobody, including the funded competitors, has shown which changes move AI answers. So the loop is also how Produkto finds out, and what it learns across every store becomes the moat (`METHODOLOGY.md`).

## What AI reads when it recommends a product

The agent has to be able to work on all five, because a product can lose for any one of them:

1. **The product page.** Plain, complete facts buyers ask about (what it is, who it's for, ingredients/materials, sizes, results, how to use, limits), proof (reviews, certifications, press), price and availability, FAQs. It must be readable without JavaScript (the 5Strands preview showed how much a theme can hide).
2. **Structured data.** Product, Offer, AggregateRating, FAQ markup in the theme.
3. **Product feeds.** Google Merchant Center feeds Google Shopping and AI Mode; ChatGPT shopping and Copilot read merchant feeds too. Feed titles and attributes often matter more than the page.
4. **The store's other pages.** Collection pages, buying guides, comparison pages, FAQ hubs.
5. **Off-site sources.** The pages AI cites for each question: listicles, review sites, Reddit threads, YouTube, marketplaces, retailers that stock the brand.

Plus the technical basics: AI crawlers allowed in `robots.txt`, a sitemap, `llms.txt`, fast pages.

The agent doesn't load all of it every time: each task gets the slice it needs (one product, its questions, its winners, its facts).

## How much it may do on its own

- **Default: drafts everything, the merchant approves** (today).
- **Trusted mode**, opt-in per change type: after a store has approved N drafts of a type without edits, those publish automatically when they pass the self-review. Undo always works.
- **Never on its own:** new claims, health or medical wording (5Strands sells health tests: regulated), prices, anything off-site.

## Build order (each step makes the loop tighter)

1. **Re-fix loop + change log** (day 39b): the agent starts learning. ~2 days.
2. **Diagnose step as its own output**: a "why you lose this question" card behind every draft. ~2 days.
3. **Technical + schema pass** with theme access: crawler access, `llms.txt`, schema, no-JS check. ~3 days.
4. **Collection pages + pages from scratch.** ~3 days.
5. **Citations workflow**: cited sources per niche → pitch drafts → track who replied. ~3 days.
6. **Feeds** (Merchant Center). ~3–4 days.
7. **Reviews + Search Console context.** ~2–3 days.
8. **Holdout experiments + METHODOLOGY.md** once 3+ stores are live. Ongoing.

## Honest limits

- Whether any of this moves AI answers is still unproven. The loop is how we'll know; until then, describe the work, never promise the result.
- AI engines update slowly: a change can take weeks to show. Verdicts need enough answers and time, or they're noise.
- Off-site work depends on other people (editors, retailers). The agent can find and draft; it can't guarantee placement.

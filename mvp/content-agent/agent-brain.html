The agent's brain, feeding itself

Every night, five collectors on the server read what the companies measuring AI search have published: competitor blogs and studies, research papers, expert posts, talks, and the engines' own documentation. The brain grades each finding by how strong its evidence is, then updates the agent's playbook or turns the finding into a test. Nobody has to feed it, and it learns from other companies' data, not only ours.

How a finding is handled

What happens to each thing that comes in

  1. Collect. Each collector runs on its own schedule and keeps only what is new since the last run.
  2. Pull out the claim. A cheap model turns each item into one checkable sentence, with its source and date.
  3. Grade the evidence. Official, Measured (with who ran it and how big), Opinion. A vendor study is marked as one.
  4. Compare. Agrees with what the brain knows, contradicts it, or is new.
  5. Act on its own. Official findings, and measured ones a second source confirms, update the playbook. Opinions become tests to run later. Competitor product launches go to a separate list.
Guardrails

What stops it learning the wrong thing

  1. One vendor study never changes the playbook alone. It needs a second, independent source. Bigger samples count for more.
  2. Opinions never change the playbook. They can only become tests.
  3. When two sources disagree, both stay on record and the disagreement goes in the Monday email.
  4. The copy rules and the "facts only from the store" rule are fixed. The brain changes what the agent works on, never what it is allowed to claim.
  5. Every playbook change is logged with the finding that caused it, so it can be undone.
Your part

What you have to do

Nothing for it to run. Optional: a short Monday email with what changed in the playbook, any disagreements between sources, and what competitors shipped that week.

Our own scans stay out of the brain: they cover too few stores and days to prove anything general. They still drive each store's own fixes.

## Mon 19 · Cheat sheet: the 4 things you control
*Educational · cheat sheet image. Title: "**AI shopping** cheat sheet". Subtitle: "What e-commerce brands can control in 2027". 4 blocks, identical structure in each: the line / 2 short points / "Do this today". Footer: photo, name, "Follow for more on AI and e-commerce".*

```
AI doesn't recommend brands.

It recommends products.

And most product pages give it nothing to work with.

When a shopper asks ChatGPT "what's the best running shoe for flat feet under $150", it doesn't care how good your Instagram is.

It needs to find a product that fits the question.

Here's what you actually control:

1. Can AI read your store?

→ If AI crawlers are blocked, nothing else on this sheet matters.
→ Product info hidden in images or pop-ups is invisible to it.

Do this today: open yoursite.com/robots.txt and check it doesn't block OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot.

2. Does your page answer the buyer's question?

→ Buyers ask with sizes, materials, prices and use cases in the question.
→ "Premium quality" answers none of them.

Do this today: write down the 3 questions a buyer asks before buying your best seller. Check your page answers each one in plain text.

3. Do other sites talk about your product?

→ AI leans on reviews, Reddit threads and "best of" roundups.
→ Your own site is only part of what it reads.

Do this today: search "best [your product type]" on Reddit and see which brands people mention.

4. Is your catalog where AI shops?

→ Google and ChatGPT both take product feeds directly.
→ If you're not in them, you're relying on the AI finding you by chance.

Do this today: check your products are approved in Google Merchant Center.

Most brands I talk to are strong on one of these and haven't looked at the other three.

Which one is yours?

Let's keep building,
Kelian

P.S. Want to see which of your products AI already recommends? Comment "CHECK" and I'll run a free check of your store.
```

## Tue 20 · The 2-minute self-check
*Short, Lily Ray style. Image: a screenshot of a real ChatGPT answer you take yourself on Tuesday morning (e.g. "best whole bean coffee under $25").*

```
Ask ChatGPT this about your best seller:

"What's the best [product type] to buy online for around $[your price]?"

Then ask it two more ways:

"Which [product type] would you recommend for [your customer's main need]?"

"[Your product type] vs [your biggest competitor's product]: which is better?"

Write down every product it names.

If yours isn't there, open the page of the product that is. Compare it to yours, line by line.

Most founders I show this to have never asked the question the way a buyer does. They've only typed their own brand name.

That tells you nothing. Your buyers don't know your name yet.
```

## Fri 23 · Pick one

**Option A: what a misread email taught us** *(story, text only)*

```
A founder replied to one of our cold emails last week.

Not to say yes. To correct us.

Our email said something like: "We asked ChatGPT for the best [product]. One of its picks was [product] by [brand]."

She read it as us mixing up her company with that brand.

Fair. The sentence never said her name. It named a rival and left her to work out why.

So we rewrote it:

"One of its picks was X by Y, a competitor of [your company]."

A few words added. The email now says why we're writing to her.

We're asking founders to trust us about something they can't see: how AI talks about their products. If one sentence confuses them, nothing after it gets read.

Write for the person who knows nothing about what you do. That's everyone.
```

## Tue 20 · Cheat sheet: 6 questions every e-commerce founder should be able to answer
*Cheat sheet image: 6 blocks, each "Question" / "Why it matters" / "How to find out". Title: "**AI search** for e-commerce founders".*

```
6 questions every e-commerce founder should be able to answer in 2027:

1. When a buyer asks ChatGPT for a product like yours, which products does it name?
→ Ask it. Takes 2 minutes.

2. Which of your products does AI recommend, and which does it never mention?
→ Usually it's a few products doing all the work.

3. Which competitor gets picked instead of you?
→ Often not the one you think. Sometimes a brand you've never heard of.

4. What do the winning product pages say that yours doesn't?
→ Read them side by side.

5. Can AI even read your store?
→ Check your robots.txt for blocked AI crawlers.

6. Who on your team owns this?
→ In most brands under 25 people, nobody does yet.

If you can't answer 3 of these, you're not behind. Almost nobody can yet.

But your buyers are already asking.
```
*"Nobody does yet" and "almost nobody can" are opinions about small teams. If Gabe would rather not generalize, change them to "often nobody" / "most founders I talk to can't".*

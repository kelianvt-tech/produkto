## 0. The thing that changes the framing

**Chris Donnelly is Co-Founder & CEO of Searchable** — "an Autonomous SEO & AEO Growth Engineer." Searchable is on our competitor list in `BUSINESS.md`.

So the playbook we've been treating as generic LinkedIn advice is a lead magnet for a direct competitor. Three consequences:

**1. It's better evidence than it looked.** He posted *"We took Searchable from 0 → $85M in 5 months"* (2,045 reactions, 386 comments). If even loosely true, a competitor built an AEO business at speed with LinkedIn as the primary channel. Our single biggest unproven risk is *"that mid-market DTC brands care enough to pay."* This is not proof — it's a public claim by an interested party, on a platform that rewards big numbers, and $85M is a valuation-or-something figure with no stated basis. But it's a signal worth chasing down properly rather than dismissing.

**2. The category-language play is already taken.** His highest-volume pillar owns exactly the vocabulary we'd want to own — AEO, GEO, SEO explainers — from an account with 1.2M followers. Competing head-on for "what is AEO" is a losing race.

**3. He is not a safe engagement target.** The skill's 30/30/30/10 rule says spend 30% on larger creators. Donnelly is a larger creator *and* a competitor. Commenting on his AEO explainers puts us in a competitor's comment section, where he sets the frame and captures the click. Engage on his LinkedIn-strategy posts if at all; stay out of his AEO posts.

**The wedge this leaves us:** he explains the category from 30,000 feet. Nobody is publishing product-page-level diagnostic data. **He owns the definition; we can own the diagnosis.** That's pillar 3 (teardowns), and it should be promoted from third priority to first.

## 1. What actually drives his account

From the 100-post teardown:

| Metric | His number | LinkedIn norm |
|---|---|---|
| Comment-to-reaction ratio | **61%** | ~6% |
| Avg comments per post | 509 | — |
| Avg reactions per post | 834 | — |
| Cadence | 9.2 posts/week, all 7 days | — |
| Format mix | 85% image "sheets", 11% video, 4% text | — |

**Five mechanics worth stealing:**

1. **The comment box is the funnel.** Gate a genuinely useful resource behind one word — "Comment PROMPT and I'll send it." One Claude-prompts giveaway pulled 2,734 comments on 1,822 reactions. Comments outnumbering likes is the tell that the mechanic is working. Each commenter becomes a DM, and the DM captures an email.
2. **Open on a number, always.** Every one of his top hooks carries a hard figure in the first line: 8,500 IKEA roles, 397,605 posts analysed, 0→$85M. Vague openers don't make his top tier.
3. **Infographic sheets carry volume, but text wins per post.** Sheets are 85% of his output at 870 avg reactions; his rare text-only posts average **1,136** — his best format. Post the sheet for saves, drop text when the idea can stand alone.
4. **Original data is uncopyable.** "We looked at 397,605 LinkedIn posts" works because nobody else has it. This is directly transferable — audit data is our version.
5. **Every post has a job.** Each one ends pointing at a resource, a webinar, or Searchable. No orphan posts.

**His seven hook types:** shocking stat · contrarian correction · original-data drop · credibility flex · milestone reveal · "I built X in Claude" reveal · free-resource giveaway.

**What he avoids:** hashtags, corporate tone, adjective-heavy claims, burying the point in a paragraph, posting without a next action.

## 2. What does *not* transfer

Worth being clear-eyed, because copying the wrong parts will hurt.

- **Cadence.** 9.2 posts a week is a full-time content operation. At pre-validation with one person, five a week is already ambitious and three is honest. Cadence is his moat, not a starting point.
- **The credibility flex hook.** "I've spent 20 years in SEO, built and sold an agency for $25M" is unavailable. Substituting a weaker version of it reads as posturing. Skip this hook entirely.
- **The milestone reveal.** No milestones exist yet. Do not manufacture them.
- **His proof-to-product funnel.** His education posts are credible *because* of the exit and the follower count. Ours have to be credible because of specificity instead — the actual attribute-level findings.

**The honest asymmetry:** he sells with authority, we have to sell with diagnosis. That's a slower engine but it's the one available, and it's more defensible against someone who can outspend and out-follow us.

## 3. Profile copy — drafts

⚠️ These are written fresh from `BUSINESS.md` / `ICP.md`. I could not read the current LinkedIn profile, so nothing below reflects what's there now. Compare before replacing.

### Headline (220 char limit)

**Option A — outcome + differentiator (recommended)**
> I help DTC brands get their products recommended by AI assistants | Not another visibility dashboard — I fix the product data that makes catalogs invisible

*154 characters.* Carries the one thing competitors don't say. "Not another dashboard" is a direct swipe at the measurement players, which is the whole positioning.

**Option B — diagnosis-led**
> Your catalog is probably invisible to ChatGPT, and your analytics won't tell you | I audit DTC product data for AI retrievability, then fix what's missing

*152 characters.* Opens on the reader's problem instead of my service. Higher stopping power, slightly more alarmist.

**Option C — expertise-led, safest**
> I audit e-commerce catalogs for AI visibility | Most product pages don't state the facts an AI needs to recommend them — I find the gaps and close them

*150 characters.* Makes no claim about outcomes at all. The one to use if the others feel like they're writing cheques the business can't yet cash.

---

### About section

> Ask ChatGPT for "a sofa with removable covers that works outdoors" and it will name two or three products. Yours probably isn't one of them — and your analytics will never tell you why.
>
> It's rarely a quality problem. AI reads stated facts, not marketing copy. Adobe found product detail pages are the least machine-readable page type on a retail site, scoring 66%. A decade of writing pages for humans and for Google means the attributes buyers actually ask about were simply never written down.
>
> I built a tool that measures this. It crawls a catalog and scores whether each product states the things buyers ask for — dimensions, materials, care, capacity, suitability — then reports which buyer queries the catalog is invisible for. On the first catalog I ran it against, 86 products scored 16.8 out of 100. Six attributes buyers actively search on appeared on exactly zero products.
>
> Then I fix them. That's the part that matters and the part nobody else does. Every other tool in this space hands you a dashboard and leaves — but nobody at a 20-person DTC brand has "action the visibility dashboard across 2,000 products" in their job description.
>
> This is for DTC brands with more products than anyone can hand-fix, in categories where buyers ask questions with numbers in them: furniture, sleep, technical apparel, tools, supplements, actives-led skincare.
>
> I'm early, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise: the tool works, the problem is measurable, and whether fixing it moves AI visibility is something I'm actively proving rather than claiming. If you want your catalog scored, comment or message me and I'll run it and send the report. No pitch attached.

**Notes on the draft:**

- Opens with a concrete query rather than a statistic — the reader can picture their own product failing it.
- The 16.8/100 is stated as "the first catalog I ran it against," never as a client or a case study, per the honesty rules. If pressed publicly, say plainly it's a family furniture shop used as a test catalog.
- The last paragraph is a deliberate risk. Admitting the remediation-to-visibility link is unproven costs some conviction and buys a lot of credibility in a category where everyone is overclaiming. If it reads as too soft, cut the clause after the semicolon — but don't replace it with a claim.
- CTA is the free audit, which doubles as demand validation toward the 20-audit milestone.

### Featured section — 3 items

1. **The attribute checklist** (PDF, gated by email) — "The 14 attributes AI checks before recommending a product." Low-friction list-builder.
2. **A sample audit report** — anonymised, showing the actual output. This is the single most persuasive asset available and it already exists.
3. **The best teardown post**, once one exists. Leave the slot empty rather than filling it with something weak.

### Banner

One line of text on a plain background, no stock imagery: **"AI recommends products it can read. Most catalogs can't be read."** Reinforces the positioning in the two seconds before anyone reaches the headline.

### Experience section

Rewrite the current role as outcome, not activity. Shape: *"Built a catalog audit tool that scores product data for AI retrievability across full Shopify catalogs — 86 products scored end to end, surfacing six buyer-critical attributes missing from every product in the catalog."*

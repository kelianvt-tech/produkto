# Webinar notes: Donnelly, "Why LinkedIn is still the #1 opportunity to scale in 2026/27"

Creator Accelerator webinar, 13 Aug 2026. Replay (unlisted): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JY9pukcAex4. Our own summary of the parts that apply to Produkto, not a transcript. The last ~30 minutes are a sales pitch for his cohort and are skipped here.

## What applies to us

**1. The delivery test has four parts.** LinkedIn decides who sees a post by checking:
- **Address:** one post, one topic, one niche. The more specific, the better it is delivered to that niche.
- **Sender:** the profile has to show the poster is credible on the topic. A post about a topic the profile doesn't mention gets little delivery. → **Every account posting about AI shopping (Kelian, Gabe, Mateo) needs a headline and About that say AI shopping / e-commerce before posting.**
- **First class:** is it worth delivering? Dwell time (how long people stay on the post), saves and shares.
- **VIP:** a track record on the topic; the poster's network already follows them for it.

**2. You don't need a big audience.** His agency's biggest client came from an inbound DM when he had ~5–6k followers, from niche content written for that exact buyer. His point: niche buyers (CFOs, founders of manufacturing companies) have quiet feeds, so whoever writes useful content for them is often the only one in their feed.

**3. Personal accounts beat company pages.** He says personal accounts convert about 20× better than a company page. Grow the personal accounts first and use them to grow the page.

**4. One focus per profile.** Someone asked about posting for three startups from one profile; his answer was don't. Mixing topics confuses both the algorithm and the reader.

**5. Posting time.** No magic hour: post when the buyer is online. He posts for UK lunchtime / US morning. → **Our buyers are mostly American: post ~9am–noon US Eastern = 15:00–18:00 Madrid.**

**6. Frequency.** He asks his cohort to post seven days a week, with a system that makes that possible. (Our plan: weekdays, one per account.)

**7. Links in posts.** He estimates a link costs a post ~10% of its reach. He accepts that when the goal is getting people into the funnel. Our default stays: link in the first comment.

**8. Capture is where most people fail.** Big followings with no revenue usually have no newsletter, no sequence, no DMs: they just hope people get in touch. Fix:
- a lead magnet good enough that people trade their email for it (if they won't, the magnet isn't good enough; a weak AI-made magnet costs trust);
- ask a question at sign-up so you can segment (he segments by customer size and interest);
- a short nurture: one of his coaches runs lead magnet (a bundle of cheat sheets) → 4-day welcome emails → free webinar → offer. Nobody goes straight from a post to buying.
- Every interaction should feel more valuable than the last one.

**9. How he makes cheat sheets.** Claude drafts the skeleton and outline; a designer builds it; he does most of the thinking himself. Rule for AI work: 10% clear brief and context up front, 80% AI does the work, 10% human polish. Owner owns every word.

**10. Where ideas come from.** Voice notes on the move (Wispr Flow), recorded customer calls (Fathom/Granola) mined for pain points and frequent questions, other platforms (he takes many from YouTube), and tools that find low-follower accounts with high-performing posts (the post worked, not the person). → **We already have Wispr Flow demo notes in `research/`: mine them for post ideas.**

**11. Compounding takes time.** His first 10 weeks: a couple of hundred followers. Judge early weeks on conversations, not reach.

**12. Sales Navigator.** Early on he'd use the cheaper Premium product; Sales Navigator once there's a funnel to feed.

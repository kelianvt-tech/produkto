Key findings

We collected 856 real AI answers to a UK skincare brand's buyer questions over three weeks and counted which products each one named. Five things stood out.

  1. 01

    Half the answers named no specific product at all. 49% gave general advice ("look for a fragrance-free cleanser") instead of recommending something you can buy.

  2. 02

    When products were named, the list was short: about four in ChatGPT and Perplexity, two in Google AI Overviews.

  3. 03

    The same question rarely got the same answer. Asked again, ChatGPT put the same brand first only 42% of the time.

  4. 04

    The engines disagreed. On the same question on the same day, ChatGPT and Perplexity shared no brand at all in 53% of cases.

  5. 05

    AI read retailers, health authorities and magazines far more than brand sites. Brand websites were 9% of citations among the 30 most-cited sites.

What this means for your store

Five practical takeaways. Which changes move visibility most is still an open question, so measure before and after each one.

  1. Measure many answers, not one

    Answers change run to run (finding 3). Track the share of answers that name each product, over weeks.

  2. Check every assistant separately

    ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google build different shortlists (finding 4). A result on one tells you little about the others.

  3. Count against answers that name products

    Half the answers recommend nothing specific (finding 1). Read your visibility against the ones that do.

  4. Win the questions where you fit best

    Each shortlist is short, but hundreds of brands get a turn (finding 2). Pick the questions your product answers better than anyone and make your page say so plainly.

  5. Look beyond your own site

    Retailers, reviewers and publishers are read more than brand sites (finding 5). Check which of them are cited for your questions, and whether they mention you.

Frequently asked questions

How does ChatGPT decide which products to recommend?

With web search on, it reads a handful of pages, such as retailer listings, reviews, publisher roundups and product pages, and names the products it can justify for the question. In our data it named about four products when it named any, and the list changed from one run to the next.

Do ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google recommend the same products?

Rarely. On the same question on the same day, ChatGPT and Perplexity shared no brand in 53% of cases, and Perplexity and Google AI Overviews shared none in 76%.

Why does AI sometimes give advice instead of naming products?

Many buyer questions can be answered with general guidance, like what ingredients to look for. In our data, 49% of answers named no specific product. That share varies by question and category.

Does my own product page matter if AI mostly cites other sites?

Yes. Your page is still where the facts about your product live, and retailers and reviewers often repeat them. But brand sites were only 9% of citations among the most-cited sites here, so what others say about your product matters too.

Is this data representative of every category?

No. It covers one UK brand's buyer questions, mostly skincare, over three weeks. Categories with strong medical or expert sources, like skincare, may cite differently from fashion or home goods.

Method
  1. Answers collected by Produkto from ChatGPT (web search on), Perplexity and Google AI Overviews, September 18 – October 8, 2026, for one UK direct-to-consumer brand's buyer questions (mostly skincare, a few about caps). The brand is not named.
  2. Product mentions were extracted from each answer with their position, brand and link. Mentions with neither a brand nor a link were treated as general advice and excluded.
  3. "Asked again" compares each answer with the next answer to the same question on the same assistant. "Same day" compares answers to the same question on the same date across assistants. Overlap is shared brands divided by all brands named in the pair.
  4. Citation shares are calculated over the 30 most-cited domains, which make up 40% of all 7,488 citations.

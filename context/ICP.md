---

## ⚠️ Read this before using any filter below

**We have zero customers. At this stage the filters govern the order we contact people — not who we accept.**

Narrow targeting before validation is a way of never discovering you were wrong. If we only audit furniture brands at 500–3,000 SKUs, we will never find out that supplement brands care three times as much. The point of the current phase is locating where the pain is sharpest, which is impossible if we've pre-decided where to look.

So: **say yes to almost anyone who will pay and let us work closely.** Revisit this file once ten customers exist and we can see where response and retention actually concentrate.

**The criterion that matters most right now isn't in any table below:** *will they talk to us every week?* Pre-product, a mediocre-fit brand with an engaged founder teaches us more than a perfect-fit brand who goes quiet after the invoice. Access and honesty beat fit until the methodology exists.

## The one-liner

A DTC brand with more products than anyone can hand-fix, enough revenue that a few thousand dollars is a rounding error, selling in a category where buyers ask questions with facts in them — and nobody internally whose job it is to fix this.

---

## Real exclusions

Only three, and none of them are about fit. These are cases where the offer is **impossible**, not merely imperfect.

- **Under ~150 SKUs.** They don't have the problem. They have an afternoon of work. Taking their money produces no case study and no learning.
- **Already a Yotpo customer.** They'll be sold Discover as an upsell and we lose on incumbency regardless of how good we are. Wasted effort, not a wasted match. Check for the Yotpo reviews widget before doing any work on a prospect.
- **Marketplace-only sellers** (Amazon/Etsy only, no own store). We can't reach their product data. Nothing to sell.

Everything else below is a **preference**, used for ordering the outreach queue.

## Preferences — what a strong prospect looks like

Use these to decide who to contact first, not who to turn away.

| Signal | Preferred | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| **Catalog size** | 500–5,000 SKUs | Big enough that hand-fixing is impossible, small enough that we can still deliver manually. |
| **Revenue** | ~$1M–$10M/year | Below $1M a few thousand dollars is a bet rather than a rounding error. Above $10M means procurement, longer cycles, and Yotpo Discover's target range. |
| **Category** | Spec-driven — see table below | Fact-shaped buyer questions give us something to make retrievable. |
| **Team size** | Under ~25, no ecommerce ops hire | Nobody in-house to run a dashboard is what makes us necessary rather than optional. |
| **Engagement** | Founder will take a weekly call | The single best predictor of whether we learn anything. |

**Deprioritise, don't exclude:** aesthetic-driven categories, brands mid-replatform, brands over $10M. If one of them says yes and will work closely with us, take it — and note in `research/` that they broke the pattern. That's a finding, not an exception.

## Category fit

| Fit | Categories | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong** | Furniture, mattresses & sleep, technical apparel & outdoor gear, tools & equipment, skincare with active ingredients, supplements, baby & kids safety products, pet products with specs | Buyers ask questions with facts in them: dimensions, capacity, material, ingredient concentration, load rating, age suitability. High consideration, often high AOV, heavy comparison behaviour before purchase. |
| **Weak** | Fashion & apparel driven by look, decorative homeware, art & prints, gifting, jewellery, impulse and low-AOV goods | Purchase is driven by aesthetics and brand affinity. Nobody asks an AI which cushion looks nicer. Nothing retrievable to fix. |

**Rule of thumb:** if you can imagine a buyer typing the question with a number or a material in it, the category qualifies.

**Our first test case sits in the strong column.** M&O Living — furniture, 86 products, 16.8/100 spec-query retrievability, six attributes stated on zero products. That's the shape of the problem in a strong-fit category.

## The buyer

⚠️ *Unconfirmed — validate in interviews.*

At $1–5M DTC, the team is small and the decision is usually founder-led.

| Role | Likelihood | What they care about |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder / co-founder** | Most likely at $1–3M | Revenue, competitive threat, not being left behind. Decides fast, no procurement. |
| **Head of Ecommerce / Marketing** | More likely at $3–10M | Their numbers, their quarterly story. Needs something to show upward. |
| **External SEO/marketing agency** | Sometimes the gatekeeper | ⚠️ May defend territory and claim they'll handle it — or may become a channel. Worth testing both readings. |

**Nobody owns this problem today.** That's precisely why it goes unfixed, and it's also our hardest objection — an unowned problem has no budget line until someone decides it's theirs.

## Trigger events

The moments that turn this from background noise into a priority. These are what cold outreach should be timed against.

- Noticed organic traffic sliding without a Google algorithm explanation
- Saw a competitor recommended by ChatGPT and not themselves *(the strongest trigger — it's personal)*
- Read or heard something about AI search and don't know where to start
- Running a broader SEO or site review anyway
- Launching a new category or a big catalog expansion
- Someone on the board or an investor asked "what are we doing about AI?"

⚠️ Log the actual trigger in every interview. The most common one becomes the subject line of every cold email we send.

## Where to find them

**Direct identification.** Shopify stores are detectable by tech stack — tools like BuiltWith, Store Leads, and Shopify's own store directories let you filter by platform, category, and traffic estimates. That's the cleanest route to a targeted list.

**Where they gather.** DTC founder communities on Slack and Discord, r/shopify and r/ecommerce, Ecommerce Fuel (paid, higher signal), 2PM, LinkedIn ecommerce circles, trade shows in the relevant vertical.

**Reverse-engineered from the problem.** Run the buyer queries for a category, note which brands *do* get recommended, then find the ones in that category who don't. Those brands are the target list, and the report writes itself — you already know who's beating them.

**Warm route.** Kelian has a second-degree contact at M&O Living — enough for an introduction, not enough to speak for the brand. Beyond that, the Barcelona and Western European DTC founder network is reachable through mutual contacts. These people will take the call and answer honestly, which makes them the right place to start — but note this is a network to be worked, not an existing book of relationships.

## The 30-second triage

Applied to any brand before spending time on them. This sorts the queue — it does not reject anyone except on the three real exclusions above.

1. Shopify? → check tech stack
2. 150+ products? → **hard floor**, count the collection pages
3. Yotpo widget present? → **hard exclusion** if yes
4. Own store, not marketplace-only? → **hard exclusion** if marketplace-only
5. Spec-driven category? → could a buyer query contain a number or a material?
6. Under $10M? → judge by team size, press, funding signals
7. Founder findable and likely to reply? → LinkedIn, they post, small team

Fails 2, 3 or 4 → skip. Otherwise audit them, and let the count of 5–7 decide who gets contacted first.

## Settled: they think product-level

**Brands do frame this at product level, not only brand level.** The wedge does not need rethinking, and this stops being an open question.

*Source: Kelian, 19 August 2026, from his own read of the market — not yet from a logged interview in `research/`. Treat as settled for planning purposes; still capture the verbatim framing in interviews, because how they say it drives copy even though what they think is now known.*

This was previously the load-bearing risk on the whole positioning — the old note read *"if nobody thinks product-level, our whole wedge needs rethinking."* It resolved in our favour. Two consequences:

- **Product-level is the pitch, not a bet.** "Win the spec-shaped queries in your category" (`00-BRIEF.md` §5) can be led with directly rather than eased into.
- **Don't re-litigate it.** Interview time that was reserved for testing this is now free for the questions still genuinely open — whether they'll pay, and what they call it.

**Not settled by this, and don't let it drift:** whether *remediation moves visibility*. That's a separate unproven, still listed in `BUSINESS.md`. Brands thinking at product level means the pitch lands, not that the fix works.

## Building the first 20 — deliberately wide

The first twenty exist to **find** the ICP, not to apply it. Spread the sample so the response rate can teach us something.

Suggested mix:

- **8 strong-fit** — 500–3,000 SKUs, spec-driven category, $2–8M. The hypothesis.
- **6 adjacent** — a different category, or much larger or smaller catalogs. Tests whether the category theory holds.
- **4 wildcards** — brands we'd assume are wrong. Aesthetic-driven, or 200 SKUs, or $15M. Cheap to include, and the most informative if one of them replies enthusiastically.
- **2 warm** — brands where Kelian already knows someone. Guaranteed honest feedback even if they never buy.

Then look at where replies actually cluster. If all the interest comes from the wildcards, the ICP above is wrong and that is the single most valuable thing we could learn this month.

Rewrite this file once ten real customers exist. Not before.
